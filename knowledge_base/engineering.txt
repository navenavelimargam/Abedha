MRPL ENGINEERING, PROCESS & MAINTENANCE DEPARTMENT
GENERAL OPERATING PROCEDURE — ENG-GEN-001
Effective Date: 01-Apr-2025 | Review Cycle: Annual | Owner: Chief Engineer

1. DEPARTMENT SCOPE
This department covers P&ID and instrumentation management, inspection and maintenance
of rotating and static equipment, process/design engineering calculations, quality control
and testing, R&D and design documentation, and equipment manuals/SOP governance.

2. INSPECTION AND MAINTENANCE FREQUENCY
2.1 Routine visual inspection (vibration, leakage, noise) of rotating equipment: every 24
    hours by the shift operator.
2.2 Detailed vibration analysis on critical pumps and compressors: every 30 days by the
    Reliability Engineering team.
2.3 Boiler and pressure vessel safety valve testing: every 90 days, mandatory, no exceptions.
2.4 Full statutory equipment inspection (boilers, pressure vessels): annually, coordinated
    with the relevant regulatory inspecting authority.

3. OPERATING LIMITS AND ALARM THRESHOLDS
3.1 Boiler operating pressure: normal range 38-42 kg/cm2; alarm at 44 kg/cm2; automatic
    trip at 46 kg/cm2.
3.2 Pump bearing vibration: alert at 4.5 mm/s RMS; trip at 7.1 mm/s RMS (per ISO 10816-3
    Class II machines).
3.3 Pump bearing temperature: alert above 80 degrees C; shutdown above 95 degrees C.

4. P&ID AND INSTRUMENTATION MANAGEMENT
4.1 All P&ID revisions must be logged with revision number, date, and the engineer who
    approved the change; the previous revision is archived, not deleted.
4.2 Any field modification to piping, instrumentation, or control logic must be reflected
    in the P&ID within 15 working days ("as-built" update requirement).
4.3 Valve tag numbers and line numbers must match the current approved P&ID revision before
    any maintenance work order is issued against that line.

5. EQUIPMENT RESTART AFTER TRIP
5.1 Root cause of any trip must be logged in the DCS event log before restart is attempted.
5.2 A pump that trips on high vibration more than once within 24 hours may not be restarted
    without Reliability Engineering sign-off.
5.3 Restart authority: Shift Engineer for a first trip; Area Manager approval required for
    a second trip within the same shift.

6. FINDING SEVERITY AND APPROVAL NOTES
6.1 Findings are classified Minor, Major, or Critical.
6.2 Minor findings are logged in the CMMS and reviewed monthly; no immediate escalation.
6.3 Major findings require Area Manager notification within 4 hours and a formal Approval
    Note to the Head of Maintenance within 24 hours, including equipment tag, finding
    description, immediate action taken, and target closure date.
6.4 Critical findings (safety or environmental risk) require immediate Plant Manager
    notification; the affected unit may be taken offline pending investigation.

7. QUALITY CONTROL AND DESIGN VERIFICATION
7.1 All engineering calculations (pressure drop, stress, capacity sizing) must be verified
    by a second qualified engineer before being used for procurement or fabrication.
7.2 QC test failures on any incoming batch must be logged and the batch quarantined pending
    root-cause review; no quarantined material may be released without QC Head sign-off.

END OF ENG-GEN-001
